Executive Capacity for Diocesan Superintendents

A veteran superintendent working quietly alongside you on the work your calendar cannot hold — not another meeting, not another voice at the table, just an extra pair of experienced hands.

The Idea

Imagine a veteran superintendent — one who spent seven years in the seat — quietly working alongside you. Not another meeting on your calendar. Not another voice at the table. Someone who takes on the work that keeps getting bumped to next week, drafts what your desk cannot draft this Tuesday, and hands it back to you finished.

Every diocesan superintendent carries more than the job description says. There is always another fire, another pastor call, another principal decision that arrived without warning. And underneath all of it — the strategic work, the drafting, the reading, the preparation — quietly slides into next week, and then the week after.

Seton's Executive Capacity engagement was built for exactly that gap. A small number of diocesan superintendents each retain a veteran peer — Dr. Vince Cascone — to carry the invisible work that no principal, no associate, and no assistant can carry for them.

The Work

Four examples of what an Executive Capacity retainer typically covers. Every engagement is shaped to the superintendent's priorities.

The Evaluation You Owe Next Friday

Reviewing, drafting, or walking through the harder principal evaluations — including the ones you have been rehearsing in your head for weeks before you have to deliver them.

The Policy That Has Not Been Touched In Five Years

Enrollment, discipline, transportation, faith formation — the diocesan policies every principal is quietly working around because they have not been revised since a different administration.

The Bishop Briefing Owed Before the Next Meeting

The strategic memo that keeps getting pushed back because there is no window in the day where it fits. Drafted for your voice, ready for your final read.

The Pastor-Principal Conversation That Hurts

A neutral outside voice to help think through the hardest relational calls in your inbox — script them, prepare for them, and in some cases carry part of them alongside you.

Other regular categories: principal search work, board and council agenda-building, accreditation prep, first-year onboarding for new principals — anything that requires a veteran superintendent's judgment and can be carried without your presence in the room.
